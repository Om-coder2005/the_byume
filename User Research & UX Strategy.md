\# 1. Purpose



This document defines \*\*who the users are\*\*, \*\*how they think\*\*, \*\*why they visit the platform\*\*, and \*\*how the interface should support them\*\*.



Unlike a traditional shopping website, this platform is centered on \*\*custom handmade products\*\*, where communication, trust, and emotional connection are just as important as functionality.



Every future design decision should align with the insights documented here.



\---



\# 2. Research Objectives



The product should answer the following questions:



\* Who are the primary users?

\* What motivates them to purchase handmade products?

\* What information do they need before placing an order?

\* What causes hesitation or abandonment?

\* How can the website reduce repetitive conversations?

\* How can the website build trust before the customer sends the first message?

\* How can ordering feel enjoyable rather than like filling a form?

\# 10. UX Design Principles



Every screen should follow these principles.



\### P1 — Calm



Avoid visual clutter.



Generous spacing.



Soft colors.



Gentle motion.



\---



\### P2 — Handmade



No generic corporate UI.



Use paper, fabric, yarn, stitched details, and subtle textures.



\---



\### P3 — Guided



Never overwhelm users with long forms.



Break complex tasks into simple steps with clear progress indicators.



\---



\### P4 — Transparent



Customers should always know:



\* What happens next

\* Estimated timelines

\* Which information is required

\* Current order status



\---



\### P5 — Delight



Microinteractions should reward actions without distracting from the experience.



Examples:



\* Yarn drawing the progress line.

\* Postcard folding animation after feedback submission.

\* Stitched checkmark after successful order placement.



\---



\### P6 — Mobile First



Since most traffic will come from Instagram, every experience should be designed for mobile before desktop.

\# 11. Information Seeking Behaviour



From research into handmade-product purchasing patterns, users typically look for information in this order:



1\. Photos of previous work.

2\. Can this be customized?

3\. Price range.

4\. Delivery timeline.

5\. Reviews.

6\. Ordering process.

7\. Artist credibility.

8\. Contact options.



This hierarchy should influence the homepage, gallery, and product pages.



\---



\# 12. Customer Psychology



Handmade purchases are emotionally driven.



Customers often value:



\* Uniqueness over low price.

\* Story over specifications.

\* Trust over speed.

\* Personal communication over automation.



The interface should reinforce these values rather than imitate a mass-market retailer.



\---



\# 13. Accessibility Goals



The platform should be usable by a wide audience.



Key objectives:



\* Sufficient color contrast.

\* Keyboard navigation for all interactive elements.

\* Screen-reader-friendly labels.

\* Clear focus states.

\* Responsive layouts from small mobile screens to desktop.

\* Meaningful alternative text for product images.



Accessibility should be considered from the beginning rather than added later.



\---



\# 14. Trust Building Strategy



Trust is especially important for custom orders.



The website should build confidence through:



\* Transparent order process.

\* Clear estimated timelines.

\* Real customer feedback.

\* High-quality photographs.

\* Progress updates during production.

\* Secure authentication.

\* Privacy-first handling of customer data.

\# 16. UX Research Assumptions



These assumptions will guide the first release and should be validated as real usage data becomes available.



\* Most visitors arrive from Instagram.

\* Mobile devices account for the majority of traffic.

\* Customers prefer visual browsing over text-heavy descriptions.

\* Custom orders require examples and inspiration before commitment.

\* Customers appreciate seeing work-in-progress updates.

\* Positive reviews significantly influence purchasing decisions.
